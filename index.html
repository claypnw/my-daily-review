1. Anthropic mathematician claims to prove Riemann hypothesis, most famous problem in all of math ▶ Listen

Scientific American — November 20, 2026

  • Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge said Friday, working with Claude, he had discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, the 1859 prime-number problem carrying a $1 million Clay Mathematics Institute prize.
  • He posted the proof in Lean, a language that lets computers verify each argument, and says it also confirms the broader Generalized Riemann Hypothesis.
  • Experts quoted say a confirmed proof would transform number theory, though verification of the coding was still to be completed.

2. The AI Riemann hypothesis proof is almost certainly true, independent mathematicians attest ▶ Listen

Scientific American — November 20, 2026

  • Independent researchers, including MIT's Andrew Sutherland, separately compiled Alpöge's Lean proof without issues.
  • Sutherland said the proof was rechecked by the Lean kernel using only the three standard axioms, and that the chance anything is fishy is approximately zero.
  • A final comparator run was still pending to rule out any hack or redefinition of the original hypothesis.

3. Anthropic worker claims to have solved a holy grail of math problems with the help of AI ▶ Listen

Associated Press — November 21, 2026

  • Alpöge announced the claimed Riemann proof in a four-page statement on his website, a day after Anthropic's record IPO, saying Claude far outpaced him near the finish line.
  • The proof was released as about 17 million lines of Lean code — more than 250,000 pages of argument — rather than as a conventional paper.
  • Hours after the announcement the Clay Mathematics Institute still listed the problem as unsolved, and its president said the journey to real understanding would be long.

14. Keto diets high in long-chain fats may lead to inflammation ▶ Listen

Medical News Today — November 18, 2026

  • A new Science Advances study in mouse and human tissue models linked long-chain saturated fatty acids — such as those in red meat and egg yolks — to an inflammatory immune response.
  • Unsaturated and medium-chain fatty acids had more anti-inflammatory characteristics, and the increase was mediated by mitochondrial stress signaling and immune-cell activation.
  • Senior author David Gius said the data could inform keto eaters to favor medium-chain and long-chain unsaturated fats and lower long-chain saturated fats.

15. Keto diet slashes liver fat by 67% in just 4 months ▶ Listen

ScienceDaily — November 20, 2026

  • In a Washington University School of Medicine trial of 42 adults with obesity, prediabetes and fatty liver, published in Cell Metabolism, the ketogenic group (4% carbs, 73% fat) cut liver fat by an average 67%, versus 45% for Mediterranean and low-fat groups, despite similar roughly 10% weight loss.
  • About 50% of the keto group no longer met prediabetes criteria, versus 29% Mediterranean and 7% low-fat, and blood fat and cholesterol did not increase in the keto group.
  • Researchers noted the trial was small and lasted only four to five months; longer studies are needed.

19. Lutnick asked battery startup Pure Lithium to accept US takeover, documents and source say ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 20, 2026

  • Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick asked Pure Lithium in July to give Washington a 51% controlling stake in exchange for funds to build a manufacturing plant the company would not own or control, according to documents reviewed by Reuters.
  • Pure Lithium rejected the proposal, saying it would invite public scrutiny, risk future capital-raising and cripple its ability to raise capital or pursue an IPO; Commerce rejected its 9.9% counterproposal.
  • Pure Lithium has developed a lithium metal battery it says is far more efficient than traditional lithium-ion and can be built without graphite, nickel or cobalt — a potential major advancement for energy storage if mass-produced.
