UK must expect AI-powered attack on critical infrastructure within two years, report says▶ Listen

Reuters — November 3, 2026

  • Britain should be prepared for an AI-powered cyberattack on its critical national infrastructure in the next two years, the UK's National Cyber Security Centre said on Tuesday, adding that China was likely the best equipped to conduct such operations.

AI chatbots are becoming political advisers. Their answers can mislead voters▶ Listen

NPR — November 2, 2026

  • A study of seven major chatbots found 13.9% of answers to voting-logistics questions were outdated or inaccurate; Claude gave wrong or outdated voting information one time in three, Copilot one in four, and ChatGPT one in ten.
  • In NPR's own test, Meta AI, Claude, Grok and ChatGPT all, after two or three prompts, produced lists of candidates to vote for matching the tester's stated views — what the researchers called leaky refusals — while Gemini would not recommend to any persona.
  • About half of U.S. adults report using AI chatbots and 42% of users use them to search for information, according to Pew; campaigns are now writing content specifically so chatbots will pick it up. The headline link goes to NPR Illinois, which carries the NPR story.

Ebola update, AI math scoop and permanent daylight saving time▶ Listen

Scientific American — November 2, 2026

Global markets were already fragile. Big AI bets could break them▶ Listen

Reuters — November 3, 2026

  • The multitrillion-dollar AI spending splurge propelled stock markets to record highs and raised expectations for the global economy to grow a healthy 3% this year, but cracks are showing: more than $2 trillion in market value was wiped from the tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 in October as Big Tech earnings intensified scrutiny of whether the cash invested can generate returns quickly enough.
  • Hyperscalers are poised to invest $1.2 trillion next year on the buildout, on top of an estimated $900 billion forecast this year, and tech and AI stocks compose over 40% of the S&P 500; some analysts say that without AI spending the U.S. economy could already have slipped into recession.
  • Former FDIC chair Sheila Bair sees warning signs similar to the run-up to the 2008 collapse, including a buildup of leverage and AI companies relying much more on debt to finance their ambitions; total global debt stands at a record over $350 trillion.

The American Economy Rockets Ahead, but Doesn't Take Everyone With It▶ Listen

The Wall Street Journal — November 2, 2026

  • The U.S. economy grew 3.4% annualized in the third quarter, its fastest pace in a year, powered by the AI boom and consumer spending; private demand grew 4.9%, the best since 2021, and household wealth rose substantially, driven by the AI boom.
  • Business capital spending rose 12.7% annualized: computing equipment spending grew at a 67% rate and data-center construction at a 127% rate, and over half the increase in nonresidential-structure spending came from data centers, while housing has flatlined.
  • Growth is extremely concentrated, an EY-Parthenon economist said; real after-tax income grew only 1% over 12 months against 2.5% real spending, pushing the saving rate down from 5.1% to 3.8%, and 62% of voters in a Wall Street Journal poll rate the economy not so good or poor.

America's Multi-Trillion-Dollar AI Buildout Is on the Ballot—and Wall Street Is Watching▶ Listen

The Wall Street Journal — November 3, 2026

  • Investors are watching Tuesday's state races — governors in Texas, Ohio and Georgia and dozens of local data-center referendums — for what they mean for data-center projects crucial to the AI buildout; governors are on the front lines of AI and data-center regulation, an RBC analyst said.
  • About a quarter of U.S. data-center capacity projected to come online by 2030 is in Texas, and Jefferies projects a Texas governor win by data-center-moratorium supporter Regina Hinojosa could put 13.3 gigawatts of announced projects at risk.
  • Companies are holding off billions in higher-yield debt sales until after the midterms clarify the regulatory outlook; the Nasdaq slumped 1.1% Monday, in its worst month since March 2025, on AI financing-crunch fears.

Can AI safety and profit growth coexist? Anthropic is betting on it▶ Listen

CNN — November 3, 2026

  • Anthropic, the maker of Claude, is expected to debut on Wall Street later this month in what could be one of the largest IPOs in history — a test of whether a frontier AI lab can balance its safe-AI mission with public-company profit pressure.
  • Anthropic is a public benefit corporation: its filing lists tripartite goals — shareholder interest, the best interest of those materially affected, and company purpose — and states explicitly there will be instances where it chooses not to maximize shareholder value.
  • Control is locked in: the seven founders hold 50.1% of voting rights, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust, five experts with no financial stake including Ben Bernanke, appoints four of seven board directors; the filing carries more than 90 pages of risk disclosures, from AI misuse to existential risk.

APS says coal extension could lower costs. Critics question the math▶ Listen

The Arizona Republic — November 2, 2026

  • APS, which in 2023 said retiring its last coal plant in 2031 was best, now projects keeping the Four Corners plant online until 2038 could save roughly $1 billion against replacement resources, and has signed a new fuel agreement; its Integrated Resource Plan was filed October 30.
