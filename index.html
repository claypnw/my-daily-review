Doctors love AI chatbots. Should health systems block — or buy in?▶ Listen

STAT News — November 5, 2026

  • Free clinician chatbots — OpenEvidence, Doximity Ask, and ChatGPT for Clinicians — have grown rapidly in the last year, used by hundreds of thousands of doctors, in part because they claim HIPAA compliance for sharing patient information after an agreement.
  • Monument Health, in South Dakota and Wyoming, blocked OpenEvidence; its chief information officer found one “super user” doctor had stored more than 100 patient records inside the tool to help make medical decisions, which had to stop.
  • Health systems are now deciding whether to block such tools or buy in, balancing day-to-day value against privacy and compliance risk.

AI could reinforce medical records’ lies about women▶ Listen

STAT News — November 6, 2026

  • Clinics and hospitals are adopting AI scribes that transcribe doctor–patient visits, and AI tools that search and synthesize records for diagnosis and treatment — but the authors’ study of 87 women found records often omitted the mistrust, misdiagnosis, and disrespect the women reported in interviews: 53 described trust-breaking encounters, only 7 of them documented.
  • If symptoms are filtered through assumptions about weight or mental health, AI may learn and reproduce that framing; one patient’s pelvic pain was attributed to obesity and mental factors for over a decade before endometriosis was finally diagnosed.
  • With complete records, the authors argue, AI could instead help identify missed-diagnosis patterns, reduce disparities, and ensure women are heard.

Japan Announces Emergency Cybersecurity Package Following a Wave of Attacks▶ Listen

Associated Press — November 6, 2026

  • Japan announced a 10 billion yen ($63 million) emergency package, including AI use and cyber rescue teams, after dozens of companies and government agencies were hit by unauthorized access and client-data leaks; Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi framed it as rapid response in the era of AI-generated attacks.
  • A “top gun” team to be set up by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry by end-November will use AI to counter hackers and detect network weaknesses, cooperating with tech companies and advanced AI developers of friendly nations.
  • The package also creates a public-private “cyber rescue team” for initial response, help for vulnerable companies, active cyber defense, and a national-security support team from the Defense Ministry, National Police Agency, and National Cybersecurity Office. 3 Charged in Scheme to Use AI to Impersonate CEOs and Attorneys to Steal $129 Million▶ Listen

Associated Press — November 5, 2026

  • Three people — Jacob Soall, 41, Sharon Hazan, 54, and Guy Sheetrit, 42 — were indicted on wire fraud, aggravated identity theft, and money laundering for allegedly targeting thousands of companies worldwide over more than a year and a half; victims included hospital systems in Missouri and California and a Rhode Island bank.
  • The scheme sent emails purporting to be from a chief executive, then used AI on phone and video calls to mimic the chief executive’s voice and sometimes image, plus a fake attorney, to direct accounting staff to wire large payments, later converted to cryptocurrency.

Kevin Roose says the race to build superintelligence is accelerating▶ Listen

Scientific American — November 6, 2026

  • A Science Quickly interview with New York Times tech journalist Kevin Roose about his book The AGI Chronicles: The Inside Story of the Race to Create an Artificial Superintelligence, tracing 2016–2026 from AI that “basically didn’t work” to systems used by billions.
  • Roose says today’s systems are solving Millennium Prize Problems in math, doing groundbreaking scientific research, and transforming jobs — a shift he likens in scale to the Manhattan Project, a comparison AI lab leaders themselves draw.
  • He argues the people most worried about AI are the insiders building it, yet they keep building more capable systems, and that regulators understand the technology but do not want to regulate it.

Could the ‘blue tsunami’ jolt the US job market back to life?▶ Listen

CNN — November 6, 2026

  • October jobs were stronger than expected and unemployment has been at or below 4.5% for a record five years, but unemployment duration is the longest in nearly five years, gains are concentrated, and wages keep falling behind inflation.
  • On AI, adoption is a frequent reason cited in layoff plans, but PNC’s Gus Faucher says those tend to overstate the labor-market impact — “AI, at least as of yet, has not been transformative for the labor market.”

Bloomberg — November 6, 2026

Data center boom could leave Americans 'on the hook' for big costs▶ Listen

USA Today — November 8, 2026

  • The Tennessee Valley Authority plans more than $13 billion in power investments through 2029, projecting 11,000 to 32,000 megawatts of added capacity by 2040; data centers already account for 18% of TVA industrial electricity demand, expected to double by 2030, and last year requested about 11,000 megawatts alone.
  • Economists warn of an AI bubble: one Columbia estimate puts U.S. data-center and related spending at $10 trillion by 2032, with about 180 gigawatts realistic against 509 gigawatts proposed; if demand does not materialize, ratepayers could pay for decades-lived plants and lines built for data centers.
  • TVA protections — an October 1 rate about 10% above the manufacturing rate, about a $1.5 million per megawatt nonrefundable capacity fee over 3 to 5 years, interruptible use — are questioned by advocates who say TVA has not disclosed enough to prove households will not subsidize the boom; industry counters that demand is strong and vacancy low.

3 Months of Carnivore After 50: What Should Have Changed▶ Listen

Carnivore Weekly — November 7, 2026

  • By 90 days, most readers over 50 have lost early water weight plus roughly 6 to 12 pounds of fat, at a typical pace of 0.5 to 1 pound a week after a 3 to 7 pound first-week water drop, with the waist coming in and afternoon energy steadied.
  • The waist is presented as the number to trust more than the scale: many women lose 1 to 2 inches off the waist in three months while the scale moves less, with the very low belly usually last to change after menopause.
  • Energy, sleep and hunger: most feel adapted by weeks 4 to 6, full fat-adaptation takes 8 to 12 weeks, sleep often improves with fewer 3 a.m. wake-ups, and hunger quieting is the biggest change — while labs, hair shedding around month three, and belly and hips often lag.

The Arizona Republic — November 7, 2026

Solar farm could harm food security, council says▶ Listen

BBC News — November 8, 2026

  • Cople Parish Council raised strong objections, in an open letter from chairman Jason Whaley, to a planned solar farm on about 260 acres of the Shuttleworth Estate near Sheerhatch Wood in Bedfordshire that could power more than 15,000 homes a year; Whaley said it would “undermine food security,” cost high-quality farmland, and harm an adjacent Romano-British settlement.
  • The farm would have 49.9 megawatt capacity; a 15 megawatt battery would store excess daytime energy, per the Local Democracy Reporting Service. Developer Quintas Cleantech called the flat, unprotected site a “great spot” and is working on more than 10 UK proposals.
  • The Shuttleworth Trust said planning matters will go through statutory channels; public consultation has closed; Bedford Borough Council first received the application in May 2025 and its planning committee has yet to decide.
