How teachers are remaking classrooms for the AI age

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • US middle and high school teachers are rethinking how they assess learning as chatbots become common: less take-home work, more in-class writing, discussion and oral exams.
  • A San Francisco sixth-grade teacher dropped take-home essays and now uses oral exams of 15 questions on character, plot, theme and metaphor.
  • School systems remain divided over AI, leaving many teachers to navigate the change largely on their own; some teachers also use AI to adapt readings and prepare class materials.

China's new export curbs draw a line around its AI crown jewels

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • China's proposed AI export curbs would keep valuable technology at home while selling access to it abroad, as rivalry with the US intensifies.
  • The draft rules, released October 16, would add 23 AI-related technologies to a restricted list, including advanced models and agents; overseas users could still use Chinese AI through websites and cloud platforms.
  • Chinese AI firms such as DeepSeek, Moonshot and Z.AI are seeking clarification on whether they can train or run their most advanced models on foreign-owned overseas servers.

AI chips worth $12.1 million seized at Malaysia airport, customs authority says

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • Malaysian customs said it had seized about 49.5 million ringgit ($12.11 million) worth of advanced AI chips this month, closing in on a suspected smuggling syndicate.
  • On October 15, authorities found 30 server units containing advanced AI chips at Kuala Lumpur International Airport's free trade zone; the servers were falsely declared as electronic components.
  • At least five cases were linked to the syndicate, involving seizures of chips worth more than 100 million ringgit in total.

India markets regulator weighs domestic AI model for securities trading, chairman says

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • India's markets regulator is working to implement a global supervisory toolkit for AI and is considering a domestically governed AI model specifically for the securities market.
  • Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said AI-enabled tools could improve services, risk assessment and supervision, with reliable data, effective oversight and clear accountability.
  • The regulator is also reviewing compliance requirements for foreign investors and will issue a consultation paper on aligning them with global standards.

OpenAI plans to buy Google's AI chips, The Information reports

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • OpenAI plans to buy Google's AI chips, The Information reported, as technology companies ramp up spending on data centers and semiconductors to meet demand for AI computing.
  • The planned purchase would deepen ties between OpenAI and Alphabet's Google, which compete in AI products and search; the report did not disclose financial terms.
  • Google's Tensor Processing Units have gained traction as an alternative to Nvidia's market-leading AI chips; Google began selling TPUs directly to some customers this year.

Nevada unions, politicians caught between AI job creation and destruction

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • East of Reno, one of the world's largest concentrations of data centers is taking shape, providing years of work for thousands of electricians, pipefitters and other union tradespeople.
  • Along the Las Vegas Strip, the AI those data centers power is taking a toll: self-driving cars replacing taxis and valets, robot bartenders, and MGM's call center slashing hundreds of jobs; by 2035, AI and automation could put up to 92,000 Southern Nevada hospitality jobs at risk.
  • The split is challenging the Nevada Teamsters ahead of the November 3 elections: candidates who did not support strong worker protections, or who backed an outright ban on new data-center construction, were unlikely to win the union's backing.

India's Waaree bets on US solar boom, expands local sourcing on data-centre growth

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • India's top solar module maker Waaree Energies is expanding manufacturing and localising supplies in the US, betting on rising power demand for data centres.
  • Waaree's US module manufacturing capacity is set to increase to 4.8 GW by the end of the fiscal year from 1.6 GW currently, and it already has an order book of more than 10 GW from the US.

Spain's Redeia increases power grid investments by 19%

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • Spain's Redeia, which controls power grid operator Red Electrica, increased investments in power networks by more than 19% in the first nine months of the year.
  • Power grid investments rose to €1.09 billion ($1.21 billion) from €915 million a year earlier; overall investments rose almost 18% to €1.14 billion and net profit grew 5.3% to €410.3 million.
  • Spain's electricity demand rose 3.3% in the first nine months of the year, and renewable energy sources accounted for 57.3% of the electricity produced in the country.

US closes $5.1 billion loan to Alliant Energy for new gas power generation, energy storage

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • The US Department of Energy closed a $5.1 billion loan package to Alliant Energy to expand power generation and bolster the grid at plants in Iowa and Wisconsin.
  • The investments will support new natural gas-fired power plants, energy storage systems and upgrades to existing generation, delivering more than 2.6 gigawatts of new or upgraded capacity — enough power for nearly 1.8 million homes.
  • Energy storage, which typically includes batteries, was supported in both the initial conditional loan and the closed loan, the department said.

Natural gas is losing its grip across key power markets

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • Across key markets in Europe and Asia, power producers are increasingly bypassing natural gas: total gas-fired generation has contracted this year in the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, Turkey, Japan, South Korea and China.
  • In Europe, higher output from renewables installations alongside battery storage networks has allowed most utilities to boost total electricity supplies while cutting overall gas generation.
  • Gas may remain indispensable for reliability, the column says, but it is increasingly valued for reliability rather than volume — an uncomfortable distinction for exporters betting on sustained growth.
