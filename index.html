OpenAI to limit weapons information for teens on ChatGPT in violence-prevention push

Reuters — October 30, 2026

  • OpenAI said on Friday it is tightening limits on the weapons-related information ChatGPT provides to teens, even when a question appears harmless, as part of new safeguards aimed at preventing real-world violence.
  • OpenAI will restrict practical information on weapons and security systems that could be misused, with the limits applying to teens even when there is no sign of violent intent. For all users, ChatGPT will be able to automatically end chats where someone appears to be fixating on violence, and OpenAI may temporarily pause accounts after multiple such chats.
  • The company also strengthened its age prediction system to move users it estimates are under 18 into the teen experience, plans to notify parents if a teen's account is paused for repeatedly discussing violence, and is limiting detail on past mass attacks, including the weapons used and how they were obtained. The safeguards begin rolling out in the next few weeks.

This retired professor was arrested for blocking a delivery robot. She says she'll do it again

CNN — October 31, 2026

  • October 14 wasn't the first time Carolyn Marvin put herself in front of an autonomous delivery robot. The 78-year-old retired University of Pennsylvania communications professor had made a habit of standing in front of the wheeled food-delivery robots in Philadelphia, delaying them for a few minutes — and on October 14 she was arrested for doing it.
  • Marvin says the case raises profound questions about public space and the legal protections extended to robots. Her concerns include the displacement of low-income delivery workers, the physical hazard the robots pose on sidewalks, and the privacy concerns posed by their embedded cameras.
  • Uber Eats launched its self-driving robot delivery fleet in Philadelphia in March, the fourth city for the service. A 2020 Pennsylvania law classifies autonomous delivery robots — "personal delivery devices" — as pedestrians, not vehicles.

The real question we should all be asking about AI

CNN — October 30, 2026

  • The stock market is turning its back on Big Tech: Google just had its worst day ever, Amazon and Meta fell significantly, and the Nasdaq tumbled to just over a percentage point away from a correction. The common thread is that building the infrastructure to power AI is very expensive, and Big Tech profits aren't yet justifying the expense.
  • Spending on AI has reached $1.6 trillion since 2023 and is expected to hit $1 trillion every single year for the next decade or so, according to JPMorgan. About a third of Big Tech's capital expenditures this year are expected to be financed with debt — something like $250 billion worth of bonds from the hyperscalers alone, according to Goldman Sachs.
  • CNN argues the real question is how much longer the bond market will sustain the nonstop spending spree, because that will ultimately dictate whether AI turns into a success or a bust. CoreWeave's bond yields surged to 15% this month as losses piled up.

Why isn't China's AI and tech boom saving its stock markets?

CNN — October 30, 2026

  • The global artificial intelligence boom that has helped sustain Chinese economic momentum this year still can't offset its domestic woes, particularly in its stock market. Since peaking in June, key Chinese indexes have fallen more than 20%, crossing the threshold for a bear market, and Beijing responded last week by pumping $19 billion into exchange-traded funds.
  • Even national tech champions could not escape the crash: Shanghai's STAR50 index, composed of China's homegrown AI and chip leaders, has plunged more than 40% since a June peak; shares in humanoid robot maker Unitree have halved since their August debut surge, and memory chipmaker CXMT's valuation has fallen by nearly 30%.
  • "The selloff reflects a repricing of crowded AI trades, weak domestic demand, and the lack of stimulus," said Gary Ng, senior economist for Asia Pacific at Natixis. Demand for AI chips and hardware worldwide has still accelerated Chinese exports this year and lifted the country to expectation-beating third-quarter growth.

Is Glendale project a data center or a power plant?

The Arizona Republic — October 30, 2026

  • A first-of-its-kind data center project in Arizona is coming to the West Valley, bringing first-of-their-kind questions about data center emissions. In 2024 an out-of-state developer bought 160 acres in the patchwork outskirts of Glendale and Surprise; unlike data center projects in the state before it, this one will be powered in part by a natural gas-fired power plant called Project Baccara.
  • The 744-megawatt power plant will bring utility-scale capability, and nearby residents worry the combined data center and power plant will bring utility-scale emissions to their neighborhoods. Experts warn that the cumulative emissions from AI infrastructure are contributing to a growing public health threat.
  • Maricopa County regulators are deciding whether to count the data center and power plant as a single source of emissions or as separate sources — a difference that could change how the pollution is regulated. The county's tentative decision is to count Project Baccara and the data center, dubbed Project Eagle, as one source of emissions, at least for now. Construction is slated to begin later this year.

A guide to swing-state energy politics ahead of US midterms

Reuters — October 31, 2026

  • Energy inflation is squeezing battleground US voters where it hurts most: their utility meters and fuel tanks. Households in the states most likely to decide control of Congress are paying $50 to $60 more a month for power and gasoline than a year ago, according to Energy Information Administration data.
  • Across Michigan, Georgia, Pennsylvania, Nevada, Arizona, North Carolina, Wisconsin and Ohio, combined household spending on power and motor fuel rose between 13% and 17% in the first seven months of 2026 from a year earlier — up $61 in Nevada and $62 in Arizona.
  • Nevada has the lowest power bills of the eight states, and prices have risen the least since 2019: massive solar farms — which can generate over half of daytime electricity supplies during the summer — have helped drive Nevada's power bills lower by displacing natural gas during daylight hours. Electricity costs continue to be pulled higher by rising consumption from tech firms and manufacturers as well as grid upgrades by utilities.

Panasonic's battery unit posts higher Q2 operating profit, lifts forecast

Reuters — October 30, 2026

  • Japan's Panasonic Holdings said on Friday that operating profit at its energy unit surged to 55.6 billion yen ($352 million) in the second quarter, from 1.2 billion yen a year earlier, boosted by higher sales of in-vehicle batteries and data centre energy storage systems.
  • Panasonic raised the unit's full-year profit forecast for the year ending March 2027 by 16%, to 198 billion yen from 171 billion yen. The company makes batteries for Tesla and other EV makers.
  • The unit's quarterly operating profit grew due to higher sales of EV batteries in North America and data centre energy storage systems, and demand from strategic customers for in-vehicle batteries continued to significantly outperform the overall market.
