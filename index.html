1. As Meta bets big on Muse AI agent, early safety mishaps surface ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 18, 2026

  • Meta's AI agent Muse has burrowed into encrypted personal messages, supplied made-up information about purchases and sent users chats seemingly meant for other people, according to internal company posts reviewed by Reuters.
  • Muse, embodied by a smiley, fuzzy mascot named Jolly, has more than 10 million downloads since its September launch and is the centerpiece of Meta's plan to generate billions of dollars in AI revenue; staff reported messages about childcare requests they had not made, product orders they had not placed and bank account balances that were not theirs.

4. Cambridge Dictionary chooses 'human' as word of the year in response to AI fears ▶ Listen

Associated Press — November 18, 2026

  • Fears of artificial intelligence inspired the Cambridge Dictionary to flip back to the 15th century for the word that best captures the 2026 cultural zeitgeist: 'human,' defined as having the qualities, faults and feelings that people have, as opposed to gods, animals or machines.
  • 'AI is changing how we talk about being human,' said Colin McIntosh, the dictionary's lexicographer, adding that fears about what humans may lose when we outsource our thinking have made us reconsider the extent to which we rely on AI.

11. Pakistan's solar revolution is so successful the national grid is lagging behind ▶ Listen

BBC News — November 17, 2026

  • Pakistan has seen a boom in imported solar panels, which glisten from the rooftops of homes and hospitals, from farmers' fields to industrial complexes; from 2018 to 2025, the share of electricity generated from solar energy in the country rose from 1% to 28%, according to think tank Renewables First, with some seven million households now owning panels.
  • Most people the BBC spoke to said they installed panels to escape soaring electricity prices from an often unreliable national grid; electricity tariffs have more than doubled in a few years, while the cost of Chinese solar panels has decreased by 93%.
  • When people buy less energy from the national grid, fewer customers are left to cover the fixed costs of maintaining it, which can force prices up and encourage even more people to switch to solar — the so-called utility death spiral.

12. Can COP31's proposed 'age of electricity' slow global warming? ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 17, 2026

  • More than 30 countries have joined COP31 host Turkey's voluntary pledge to boost global electrification; Turkey hopes the pledge will raise power's share of final energy demand worldwide to 35% by 2035, from just over a fifth today.
  • Electrification, particularly using domestic renewable energy and batteries, can help reduce import dependence and exposure to fossil fuel price shocks, COP31 leaders have said.
  • In sunny regions, the lifecycle cost of building and operating solar-plus-batteries projects ranges from $54 to $82 per megawatt hour, according to the International Renewable Energy Agency, and managing fluctuating wind and solar output requires massive power grid upgrades and battery storage investments, analysts said.
