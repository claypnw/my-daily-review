Human rights activists look to harness AI for the greater good

NPR — October 27, 2026

  • At a Human Rights Foundation hackathon in Washington, D.C., 10 human rights activists spent two days building working tools with AI coding agents.
  • Zimbabwean pastor Evan Mawarire built a scheduler tool to help activists plan rest breaks; Carine Kanimba's "Behind the Jersey" site, which tracks the human-rights records of sports sponsors, won the event and is already live.
  • The event showed that people with no coding background can now build usable software for their causes in days, using AI agents.

Disinformation videos, likely from Russia, are exploiting real people to malign Senate candidates

Associated Press — October 28, 2026

  • Social media videos believed to be produced with artificial intelligence and linked to Russia are misusing real people's names and photographs to spread disinformation about two Democratic Senate candidates in the final stretch of midterm voting.
  • One video targets Sherrod Brown of Ohio and pictures Haya Akbik, who interned for Brown several years ago; she told the Associated Press the claims attributed to her are "completely made up." A second video targets James Talarico of Texas, framed as a direct-to-camera confessional from a former student.
  • X removed the initial posts, but copies remain online; the videos show how AI magnifies a familiar foreign-interference playbook by making fabricated information appear very real.

Australians should prepare for online services to go down unexpectedly as threats rise, cyber agency warns

The Guardian — October 26, 2026

  • Australia's signals intelligence agency, the ASD, says in its annual cyber threat report that hostile foreign governments, criminal groups and individual hackers are using AI tools to gather intelligence, interfere with essential infrastructure and steal money.
  • The agency responded to 1,500 cybersecurity incidents in 2025–26, up 20 per cent, and alerted outside organisations to more than 4,400 potential malicious incidents, up 80 per cent in a single year.
  • The ASD argues that system security must be prioritised over system availability — services may be taken offline deliberately while they are secured — and that defenders need advanced AI tools to stay ahead of AI-enabled attackers.

UK met 137 businesses and no environmental groups in shaping AI datacentre policy, investigation finds

The Guardian — October 27, 2026

  • An investigation by the nonprofit Spotlight on Corruption, built on 40 freedom of information requests to 18 government agencies, found that ministers and senior officials developing the UK's AI growth zones policy met 137 businesses, just two community groups, and no environmental organisations.
  • The same investigation found that 38 ministers, senior civil servants and special advisers moved into jobs at AI companies between July 2024 and September 2026, nine of them from the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology.
  • The growth zones are meant to spur huge new datacentres across five regions of the UK; campaigners say the policy was shaped almost entirely by the industry that profits from it.

How teachers are remaking classrooms for the AI age

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • US middle and high school teachers are rethinking how they assess learning as chatbots become common: less take-home work, more in-class writing, discussion and oral exams.
  • A San Francisco sixth-grade teacher dropped take-home essays and now uses oral exams of 15 questions on character, plot, theme and metaphor.
  • School systems remain divided over AI, leaving many teachers to navigate the change largely on their own; some teachers also use AI to adapt readings and prepare class materials.

AI Cybersecurity Startup Upwind Raises at $3.8 Billion Value

Bloomberg — October 28, 2026

  • Upwind Security, a San Francisco startup offering cybersecurity for artificial intelligence and cloud applications, has secured $400 million in fresh financing that values the company at $3.8 billion.
  • Bessemer Venture Partners led the investment, with Greylock Partners and Craft Ventures also taking part; the company has raised $830 million since its founding in 2022 and was last valued at $1.5 billion in January.
  • Upwind's platform detects threats in companies' cloud infrastructure, such as malware or suspicious activity — demand for guarding AI systems is itself becoming a business.

What is the 'AI bubble' and what happens if it pops?

USA Today — October 27, 2026

  • An "AI bubble" is the idea that financial markets have overvalued AI companies through overinvestment that will not see a positive return, eventually creating market instability.
  • Dennis Kelleher of Better Markets warns that if the bubble bursts, "the economy and Main Street Americans will suffer" through lost money, unpaid loans, bankruptcies and a contracting economy; author Rob May counters that there are "pockets of bubbles" rather than one violent pop, and predicts investors will stop pumping money into AI startups and model labs within about 18 months.
  • Anthropic's initial public offering, filed October 26, is a live test: the company reported $16.3 billion in first-half revenue but has committed to more than half a trillion dollars in computing costs.

Broadcom and other AI stocks lift Wall Street closer to its record

Associated Press — October 27, 2026

  • Stocks in the artificial-intelligence industry led Wall Street higher on Tuesday: the S&P 500 rose 0.6% to within 1.2% of its all-time high, the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 16 points, and the Nasdaq composite climbed 1.2%.
  • Broadcom climbed 4% after announcing a $63 billion financing deal in which several Wall Street firms will help Anthropic get access to Broadcom's XPU AI accelerators and networking equipment; Nvidia added 0.9% and CoreWeave jumped 7.9%.
  • Most stocks in the S&P 500 actually fell — the gains of a few influential AI stocks more than made up for it, after weeks of swings over how expensive it has become to borrow money to build data centers and buy AI chips.

California communities are fighting back on data centers

USA Today — October 26, 2026

  • California jurisdictions across the state have moved to restrict data center development: Oakland, San Francisco and South El Monte each passed 45-day moratoriums in October, Los Angeles County barred large-scale data centers that power AI in its unincorporated areas, and Alhambra voters will decide on November 3 whether to affirm their city's ban.
  • California has more than 200 active data centers, and earlier this year Governor Gavin Newsom signed bills requiring data centers to report energy, water and fuel use, with rules on expansion and energy costs.
  • The pushback comes as a majority of California adults oppose building data centers to support AI in their areas, according to a Public Policy Institute of California survey; nationwide, at least 16 states have taken action to halt data centers.

Nevada unions, politicians caught between AI job creation and destruction

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • East of Reno, one of the world's largest concentrations of data centers is taking shape, providing years of work for thousands of electricians, pipefitters and other union tradespeople.
  • Along the Las Vegas Strip, the AI those data centers power is taking a toll: self-driving cars replacing taxis and valets, robot bartenders, and MGM's call center slashing hundreds of jobs; by 2035, AI and automation could put up to 92,000 Southern Nevada hospitality jobs at risk.
  • The split is challenging the Nevada Teamsters ahead of the November 3 elections: candidates who did not support strong worker protections, or who backed an outright ban on new data-center construction, were unlikely to win the union's backing.

Homes across UK to get low- or no-interest loans to fund solar panels and heat pumps

The Guardian — October 27, 2026

  • Households across the UK can now cut their energy bills by hundreds of pounds a year at no upfront cost under the government-backed Warm Home Loans scheme, which launched on Tuesday, by applying for interest-free or low-interest loans for upgrades such as heat pumps, batteries and solar panels.
  • The loans range from £7,500 to £35,000; the government estimates that installing a heat pump, battery and solar panels could save households up to £550 a year on energy bills, rising by a further £300 a year with a time-of-use tariff.
  • Energy suppliers including British Gas and E.On have partnered with lenders to offer the loans directly to customers at a discounted rate.

US closes $5.1 billion loan to Alliant Energy for new gas power generation, energy storage

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • The US Department of Energy closed a $5.1 billion loan package to Alliant Energy to expand power generation and bolster the grid at plants in Iowa and Wisconsin.
  • The investments will support new natural gas-fired power plants, energy storage systems and upgrades to existing generation, delivering more than 2.6 gigawatts of new or upgraded capacity — enough power for nearly 1.8 million homes.
  • Energy storage, which typically includes batteries, was supported in both the initial conditional loan and the closed loan, the department said.
