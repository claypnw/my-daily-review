Why Anthropic is turning away some users

Reuters — October 22, 2026

  • Anthropic has cut off a meaningful slice of paying customers — between 5% and 7% of its revenue in the first nine months of the year — because, it alleged, those accounts were Chinese AI labs quietly mining Claude to train their own models.
  • The technique is distillation: a smaller “student” model learns by studying the outputs of a larger “teacher” model. Anthropic said in February that Chinese labs including DeepSeek, Moonshot and MiniMax had generated more than 16 million interactions with Claude through roughly 24,000 fake accounts.
  • The company rolled out new defenses earlier this month — access restrictions, proxy blocking, and bans on tens of thousands of accounts — which it says has cut off 90% to 99% of the data being siphoned, and it is telling investors the crackdown has effectively stopped Chinese developers from distilling its models.

Chase & Status on the upside of AI for music

Reuters — October 24, 2026

  • British electronic duo Chase & Status say their new album “Generic Everything” emerged from conversations about artificial intelligence, social media and the pressure for artists to follow established formulas rather than forge their own path — and they reject the idea that AI poses an existential threat to creativity.
  • Saul Milton said the fear is the complacency AI enables: “Suddenly you can put in a prompt, type a few words and it spits something out,” and that ease can squash creativity. He compared the shift to earlier moves from vinyl to downloads to streaming, after which people adapted.
  • Asked what AI cannot replicate, Milton said: “Original ideas. Fundamentally, it's recycling ideas. It can make things polished, but it can't really think differently. Style is individuality.”

Cable capacity emerges as bottleneck for AI data centres, Nexans says

Reuters — October 22, 2026

  • Cable production capacity is emerging as a bottleneck for AI-driven data centre construction, as demand rises faster than the industry's ability to expand manufacturing capacity, Nexans' finance chief Vincent Piquet told Reuters.
  • Global cable capacity is tight and building a new cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE) cable production line takes around three years, limiting how quickly suppliers can respond; data centres require large amounts of low- and medium-voltage cable to connect power infrastructure.
  • The strain is already delaying some projects — in September, Oracle issued a force majeure notice to a Blue Owl unit developing a large data centre in New Mexico, citing potential difficulties securing sufficient power — and French operator Enedis has moved to contracts lasting six or seven years to secure cable supply.

Why AI data centers are powering a boom in fuel-cell demand

Reuters — October 23, 2026

  • Fuel cells are gaining traction among US hyperscalers racing to build data centers for the AI boom but struggling with grid constraints to secure quick and reliable power; Rystad Energy projects investment in fuel cells for data centers to rise to about $30 billion by 2030 from $2.8 billion in 2025.
  • Linking large electricity loads to the US grid can take three to six years, prodding developers to explore on-site power; Bloom Energy said it delivered power to an Oracle site in Utah in less than two months, and Rystad predicts about 40% of projected US data-center capacity in 2030 could pursue dedicated on-site power.
  • Bloom's third-quarter revenue surged 138% to $1.24 billion and it raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $4.4 billion to $4.6 billion; Oracle intends to procure up to 2.8 gigawatts of Bloom fuel-cell systems, of which 1.2 GW has been contracted.

Data center spending to more than double to $2.1 trillion by 2028, BofA says

Reuters — October 23, 2026

  • Global data center spending is expected to hit $1 trillion in 2026 and more than double to $2.1 trillion by 2028, BofA Global Research estimated in a note on Friday, driven by growing demand for and development of AI services.
  • AI is seen driving the expansion, which the brokerage says will fuel a 37% market-wide compound annual growth rate from 2025 to 2030; the data center market, worth $275 billion in 2022, could reach $2.93 trillion by 2030.
  • BofA put data center construction costs at $49 million per megawatt, with IT-related expenses making up 78% of total spend; servers are the costliest component at $31.4 million per megawatt, and chipmakers and server developers have been among the biggest beneficiaries, particularly in the US.

The Week in Breakingviews: Data dreams delayed

Reuters — October 24, 2026

  • Columbia Business School's Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh estimates the AI boom will produce 183 gigawatts of data centre capacity in the United States alone by 2032, almost five times the pre-2025 figure, costing more than $10 trillion and amounting to more than 3.5% of US GDP over the period.
  • In the past few weeks that vision has dimmed: investors are questioning the financial viability of so-called neoclouds, rising borrowing costs have squeezed CoreWeave's margins, rival Firmus Technologies cancelled its initial public offering, and delays in accessing the electricity grid prompted Microsoft to walk away from a UK lease with Nscale.
  • Reuters Breakingviews notes that multiple US states have imposed moratoria on new data centre construction, and that a world with fewer, more expensive AI data centres would force chipmakers, equipment suppliers, AI users and financiers to recalibrate.

China urges US to halt proposed expansion of outbound investment curbs

Reuters — October 24, 2026

  • China's commerce ministry urged the United States on Saturday to stop expanding restrictions on American investments in China, saying a proposed rule on sensitive technologies would disrupt trade ties.
  • The US Treasury Department issued proposals on Thursday to update its outbound-investment security programme, adding biotechnology to restricted sectors and expanding coverage of high-performance computing and hypersonic systems; the programme had already restricted certain US investments involving China in semiconductors, quantum technologies and artificial intelligence.
  • Beijing said the proposal “discriminatorily” widened China-related investment restrictions, firmly opposed it, reserved the right to take countermeasures, and said it would damage bilateral economic ties.

2 tornadoes confirmed in Arizona, including in Heber-Overgaard

The Arizona Republic — October 24, 2026

  • Two tornadoes touched down in Arizona on Wednesday, October 21, during a rare and powerful severe weather outbreak; the National Weather Service released storm surveys on October 24 ranking the Heber-Overgaard tornado an EF1, with wind speeds of 110 mph and a path 350 yards wide.
  • From touchdown at 6:59 p.m. it traveled about 9.25 miles in 11 minutes across State Route 260 through Heber-Overgaard, damaging homes, trees and a commercial structure; there were no fatalities, and only one person was sent to the hospital with injuries that were not life-threatening.
  • The other tornado hit Yavapai County on Spruce Mountain between 7:32 and 7:46 a.m., traveling about 8 miles, snapping or uprooting ponderosa pines; it was also EF1, with maximum winds of 105 mph and a path about 300 yards wide.

National Weather Service confirms EF1 tornado in Navajo County following severe storms

FOX 10 Phoenix — October 24, 2026

  • The National Weather Service confirmed a tornado touched down in the Heber-Overgaard area on Wednesday evening, causing significant damage; on the evening of October 21 an EF1 tornado moved through Navajo County around 7 p.m., damaging several homes and buildings and uprooting and snapping trees.
  • According to the official NWS report dated October 24, winds peaked at 110 mph, with a path of 9.26 miles and a width of 350 yards, and the event lasted 11 minutes; one person suffered a non-life-threatening injury.
  • Officials also determined an EF1 tornado occurred near Lookout Mountain on the morning of October 21, bringing peak winds of 105 mph, with a path length of 8.02 miles and a width of 300 yards, and no injuries reported there.
