1. United Airlines bans personal AI agents from booking flights ▶ Listen

USA Today — November 11, 2026

  • United Airlines announced on November 11 that it is banning personal AI agents from booking flights, saying the move protects customer data and keeps the airline in line with FAA and DOT rules.
  • United said the agents can expose sensitive information, and that actions taken by these tools may result in unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles; agents can still show United flight options and prices, but cannot complete a purchase.
  • Meta's Muse agent passed 10 million users in October; banks restricted AI agents in October and Amazon blocked them in September, and flight check-in still requires a human to confirm that bags hold no banned substances.

2. How AI is reshaping the fight over banned books ▶ Listen

USA Today — November 12, 2026

  • A PEN America report released for Banned Books Week in early October warns that using AI to guide book removals will worsen the snowball effect of school book bans.
  • Some districts have had AI review their collections and flag content; critics say the tools hallucinate and pull passages out of context, and one Florida district's AI scan sent more than 1,800 titles for further review in October.
  • PEN America counted nearly 12,000 book bans in the 2025–2026 school year, with Texas and Florida making up more than 11,400 of them; Moms for Liberty announced an AI tool in October to scan school board meeting agendas, with human review.

3. AI has made it 'close to impossible' to assess university coursework, regulator says ▶ Listen

The Guardian — November 11, 2026

  • England's Office for Students said universities should return to traditional exams and in-person presentations after lecturers said AI has made it 'close to impossible' to assess coursework.
  • The finding comes from an OfS and Advance HE report that interviewed more than 300 students and staff at 127 universities and colleges; the OfS said the credibility of degrees is under threat.
  • The regulator warned that failing to design valid and reliable measures of students' knowledge and skills could breach conditions of registration and lead to sanctions, and its strategy director called AI use 'a generational risk.' 4. Can Paris Hilton's AI shopping assistant help you save more on Black Friday? ▶ Listen

USA Today — November 11, 2026

  • Mate, a free AI shopping assistant backed by Paris Hilton, launched ahead of Black Friday to help shoppers save more.
  • The assistant finds products, negotiates discounts and guaranteed cashback at more than 200,000 U.S. stores, and can handle checkout and track packages.
  • There is no separate app: Mate works by text through iMessage or WhatsApp, including from a photo of something the shopper wants.

7. AI to stay dominant force behind US business investment in 2027, Goldman says ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 12, 2026

  • Goldman Sachs forecasts annualized spending on AI-related hardware and intellectual property will climb to $1.4 trillion by the end of 2027 — nearly 4% of the U.S. economy — and pass $900 billion by the end of 2026.
  • Goldman estimates AI investment will directly add 5.3 percentage points to 2027 business investment growth, including semiconductor investment, and 0.6 percentage points to U.S. GDP growth.
  • Overall U.S. business investment growth is forecast at 8% in 2027, nearly double the 4.2% consensus, and Goldman said the Democrats' midterm victories pose only limited risks to AI investment.

9. Men see more career advancement in jobs with AI, as women lose ground ▶ Listen

The Washington Post — November 9, 2026

  • The Washington Post reports that men are seeing more career advancement than women in jobs where artificial intelligence tools have been introduced.
  • The pattern is tied to jobs that already use AI tools for work such as writing, coding, scheduling and customer service, rather than roles that might be automated years from now.
  • The reporting states the outcome but does not confirm a cause; labor researchers generally point to who gets access to new tools and training first, and to men and women clustering in different job types and seniority levels. 12. AirTrunk, European Energy Australia sign exclusivity deal on Hunter renewables projects ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 12, 2026

  • AirTrunk and European Energy Australia signed an exclusivity agreement to develop two renewable projects in New South Wales' Hunter region, inside the Hunter-Central Coast Renewable Energy Zone.
  • The projects comprise a 260 MW solar project, a battery of more than 800 MWh and up to 350 MW of wind; the solar and battery parts are expected to begin operating from 2030 and the wind from 2032.
  • The companies are discussing a long-term power purchase agreement to support AirTrunk's energy needs in New South Wales, including its proposed data centre campus at the former Kurri Kurri smelter site; AirTrunk aims to match all its electricity use with renewables by 2030.
