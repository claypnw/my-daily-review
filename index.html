Trump says AI risks can be managed, signals openness to guardrails ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 9, 2026

  • President Trump told CBS's "60 Minutes" that he "doesn't mind guardrails" on AI and that the risks of the technology can be managed, signalling openness to working with Democrats on rules.
  • He said he does not want the United States to fall into a "deep second position" behind China in AI.
  • The comments mark a shift: the president had previously favoured letting the AI industry police itself.

Jensen Huang: Silicon Valley superstar engulfed by personal scandal ▶ Listen

BBC News — November 9, 2026

  • Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation after reports that chief executive Jensen Huang had a relationship with a woman American intelligence officials believe was recruited to spy for China — Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore data centre company Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer.
  • The board said it has no reason to believe American national security or Nvidia's secrets were compromised, and people familiar with the matter told the BBC Huang's removal is unlikely; Nvidia said it had not been aware of intelligence concerns about the relationship.
  • Huang admitted the affair in a statement on Sunday, saying he had been unfaithful to his wife and took full responsibility. The headline link goes to a full copy of the BBC News story carried by World Right Now.

‘AI clog’: UK public bodies battle onslaught of chatbot-generated complaints ▶ Listen

The Guardian — November 8, 2026

  • UK councils, MPs' offices, courts and schools are being flooded with complaints and requests drafted by chatbots — a problem staff now call "AI clog"; a senior caseworker in a Labour MP's office, Hollie Wickens, estimates about half the emails the office receives show signs of AI drafting.
  • A typical complaint used to run about two pages; most now run about ten times that length, and one reached about 200 pages, according to Deborah Evans of Lawyers in Local Government. Researchers at the Hertie School, the Cooperative AI Foundation and GovAI call the pattern "agentic flooding."
  • Transport for London logged a record 4,769 freedom of information requests in 2025-26. The headline link goes to a full copy of the Guardian story carried by World Right Now.

Staff at the US center for AI safety are reporting to the White House, raising independence concerns ▶ Listen

Associated Press — November 8, 2026

  • Staff at the Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI), the Commerce Department unit inside NIST that tests frontier AI models, were recently asked to report informally to an office of the White House, according to two former officials who spoke to the Associated Press on condition of anonymity.
  • The centre has no permanent director — one director was hired and fired within days in April, and his replacement resigned in July — and it has stopped publishing its research; its budget is about $10 million a year. Executive Order 14409, which governs federal pre-release AI testing, does not mention CAISSI and sets no requirement to publish assessments.
  • The White House did not answer specific questions about CAISSI; it said President Trump had formally established his Super Intelligence Force and commissioned a risk report from his Council of Advisors on Science and Technology. The headline link goes to the Associated Press wire copy of the story carried by the Plain Dealer site.

Tech set to still sway US stock market's course despite AI-driven swoon ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 9, 2026

  • Technology stocks still make up more than 35% of the S&P 500, even after the biggest one-day drop in tech shares in over six years, so the sector will keep steering the wider market.
  • Tech's forward price-to-earnings ratio fell to 17.1, below the broader S&P 500's 17.9 for the first time since 2017, while doubts grow around Anthropic's planned IPO.
  • Earnings ahead will test the mood: CoreWeave reports Tuesday, Nvidia on November 17 and Dell on November 24.

NPR's 'Machine Gods' podcast: What the midterms could change about AI Policy ▶ Listen

NPR — November 6, 2026

  • On NPR's "Machine Gods" podcast, Kevin Roose and Casey Newton work through what the midterm results could change about AI policy, after voters in several places approved data-center bans and restrictions.
  • Most of the computing capacity for 2027 is already built or under construction, so the real fight is over the 2028 capacity that has not been started; if US building gets harder, the labs may build outside the country.
  • Newton is doubtful the results will pace the frontier labs, noting that the last period of unified Democratic government brought no meaningful tech regulation.

What's going on with the stock market? ▶ Listen

NPR — November 6, 2026

  • NPR's "Consider This" looks at a wild week: the AI boom has been a major boost to the stock market, but investors fear the midterm blue wave will bring increased scrutiny of data centers and the AI they support.
  • Stocks soared on Wednesday, then a sell-off in AI shares on Thursday sent the tech-heavy Nasdaq down more than 5% and wiped out more than $2 trillion in market value; shares bounced back some on Friday, but investors remain jittery.
  • The programme asks whether the market is in the middle of a tech bubble that is about to burst.

Cramer's week ahead: AI data center stocks face another hurdle and so does the Fed ▶ Listen

CNBC — November 6, 2026

  • CNBC's Jim Cramer said the coming week's earnings from neocloud companies could test investor confidence in the battered data center trade: CoreWeave reports Tuesday and Nebius on Thursday, with Constellation Energy on Monday.
  • Two inflation readings also land this week — the consumer price index on Tuesday and the producer price index on Friday — and Cramer warned that stubbornly high prices could prompt Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh to raise interest rates again.
  • The Fed raised rates in September for the first time in three years. The story was read in a CNBC copy carried by the Daily Wire bulletin; the headline link goes to the CNBC original.
