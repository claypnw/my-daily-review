Bulgaria emerges as a surprise leader in Europe's quest for battery storage

Reuters — October 23, 2026

  • Helped by €700 million in European Union grants, Bulgaria has doubled its battery capacity over the last year — to around 6 gigawatts, up from 3 gigawatts — allowing it to store solar energy produced in the day and release it during peak hours in the evening, earning the nickname the "Battery of the Balkans."
  • Bulgaria increased its power exports by 62% this year, to 3.5 terawatt-hours, supplying neighbouring countries when heatwaves in July and August left national grids struggling; it has exported as much as 50,000 megawatt-hours of electricity per day.
  • Greece, by contrast, is lagging in the battery race despite its sunshine: its total storage is expected to reach only one gigawatt by the end of the year, even though its solar capacity more than tripled between 2020 and 2025.

China's Sigenergy to build inverter, battery storage plants in India

Reuters — October 23, 2026

  • Chinese energy storage systems maker Sigenergy plans to invest an initial $250 million in India to set up manufacturing facilities for inverters and battery energy storage systems.
  • The company will first build an inverter manufacturing plant expected to be operational by the second quarter of 2027, and a battery energy storage systems factory six to eight months after that; it also plans a research and development centre in India focused on battery energy storage.
  • India is looking to rapidly expand renewable energy capacity and grid-scale energy storage to support its clean energy goals, creating opportunities for manufacturers.

India's Adani Green Energy posts quarterly profit rise on higher power sales

Reuters — October 21, 2026

  • India's Adani Green Energy reported a nearly 16% rise in second-quarter profit, driven by higher power sales; revenue from power supply jumped 45% and energy sales rose 33%.
  • The company said it could exceed its full-year outlook of 10 gigawatt-hours of battery energy storage capacity, after installing 3.08 GWh of capacity at its Khavda site in Gujarat during the quarter; its total operational storage capacity is currently 6.63 GWh.
  • Its operational renewable capacity stood at 20.8 gigawatts at the end of September, up 24% from a year earlier, and the company aims to reach 50 GW by 2030.

Polish power regulator wants wider EU battery rule exceptions to ease grid struggle

Reuters — October 20, 2026

  • Companies running Poland's local power grids should be allowed to own batteries where they are needed to stabilise the network, the head of the country's energy regulator told Reuters, calling for wider exceptions to the European Union's energy storage rules.
  • Poland's power system is struggling to keep up with more than 1.6 million small solar installations, as grid-ordered cuts to renewable output more than doubled in 2025 to nearly 1.4 terawatt-hours; "Without more storage and its flexibility, it will be very difficult, if not impossible, to lower prices," the regulator said.
  • EU rules generally bar grid operators from owning batteries so they cannot compete with private investors; Poland allows exceptions only for batteries the regulator deems integral to the grid.
