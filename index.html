1. Kazakhstan-linked hackers used AI to steal Tajik government documents, researchers say ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 11, 2026

  • A hacking group tied to Kazakhstan, nicknamed "Silent Lynx," used AI to steal more than 2,000 national security-related documents from neighboring Tajikistan, according to Singapore-based cybersecurity firm CloudSek.
  • CloudSek said the spies used Hermes, an open-source framework for autonomous AI agents, after inadvertently leaving a server exposed; the AI planned attacks, wrote rules, ran them, and wrote up reports instead of hackers typing commands by hand.
  • The hackers used Hermes for reconnaissance from around mid-August and broke into Tajikistan's electronic document system a couple of weeks later, with the AI writing a custom program to download and organize the files.

3. Food delivery habits are giving cybercriminals personal info ▶ Listen

USA Today — November 10, 2026

  • Artificial intelligence is advancing cybercriminals' ability to gather and analyze personal information from food delivery orders and online shopping receipts, according to research released this month by Cisco Talos.
  • The Windows-based malware called "VOLTA-AUTODOXER" is augmented by a large language model that sorts through passwords, payment cards, WiFi profiles and conversations, and builds a victim profile that can include geolocation, home address, employer and inferred birth year.
  • Researcher Ryan Fetterman said this is the first time AI has been used to augment malware in this way, and that the AI integration prompts the model to guess what a potential victim is worth and assess security posture and password hygiene.

4. AI phishing scams turn your social posts against you ▶ Listen

Fox News — November 10, 2026

  • Researchers at the University of Texas at Arlington found that widely available AI tools can gather personal details from public social media accounts and turn them into highly convincing personalized phishing emails; as few as 10 posts from one person provided enough information.
  • The team studied 200 public Instagram accounts and evaluated more than 18,000 AI-generated emails; in a user study, participants generally rated the AI-generated emails as more convincing and less suspicious than real-world phishing examples.
  • Three widely used AI safety tools — Llama Guard, ShieldGemma and WildGuard — detected only 0% to 19% of requests to create personalized phishing attacks at their default settings, though adjusted safeguards reached detection rates as high as 99% in the researchers' tests.

5. Is AI deciding who's dangerous in Michigan prisons? ▶ Listen

Fox News — November 10, 2026

  • Michigan plans to begin testing a new prisoner classification system on December 1 at selected men's prisons; the Michigan Department of Corrections says the redesigned system is expected to improve its ability to predict future violence by approximately 30%.
  • The software comes from Recidiviz, a nonprofit that supports criminal justice agencies in 19 states; it calculates security scores from prison records using a points-based formula chosen by Michigan corrections experts, and Recidiviz says AI plays no role in calculating the scores — corrections staff make the final decisions and can override a score.
  • The new system allows ongoing reassessment as prison records update, instead of reviews only at scheduled intervals; the corrections officers' union president warned the bigger problem is a shortage of higher-security beds, and the state plans 870 high-security beds under its Safe Prisons Initiative.

9. Carnivore in Senior Living: Ordering From the Dining Room ▶ Listen

Carnivore Weekly — November 10, 2026

  • The article says carnivore is doable in independent or assisted living because dining rooms already serve meat, eggs and fish daily; it advises starting with the dietitian or dining manager, not the server, and asking to swap vegetables and starches for extra protein.
  • It recommends a standing order on file — protein in place of starch and vegetables at every meal, butter on the side, eggs at breakfast, and a plain-protein backup such as grilled chicken, a hamburger patty, or eggs for pasta and casserole nights — aiming for roughly 30 to 40 grams of protein per meal.
  • For chewing and social ease, it lists soft options such as scrambled eggs, ground meat, braised beef, fish and strained stews, checking the week's posted menu on Sunday, and using a one-liner at a shared table: "I just feel better eating simply."

13. India bans export of lithium-ion battery recycled material ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 11, 2026

  • The Indian government on Tuesday banned the export of lithium-ion battery black mass, a recycled battery material containing metals such as lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese, changing its export status from "free" to "prohibited" with immediate effect.
  • The ban prohibits Indian recyclers from exporting lithium-ion battery black mass and associated unsorted battery waste that does not contain lead, cadmium or mercury, encouraging domestic processing of critical battery materials and supporting India's electric-vehicle supply-chain ambitions.
  • The notification comes days after the Indian cabinet approved an incentive policy worth 28.14 billion rupees ($300.2 million) to boost domestic processing of lithium and nickel; India is targeting electric vehicles at 30% of new car sales by 2030.
