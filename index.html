1. AI is disrupting math. Is science next? ▶ Listen

Scientific American — November 12, 2026

  • In May, OpenAI models guided by humans disproved a long-standing conjecture posed by Paul Erdős, in August AI models effectively solved the Navier-Stokes problem, and in October OpenAI released 722 AI-generated preprints on some 370 open problems, including a partial solution to the Riemann hypothesis.
  • Scientists now routinely use AI for coding and increasingly for analysis, and in late October OpenAI and Anthropic both announced progress on a thorny problem underpinning the math of the Standard Model of particle physics.
  • Researchers interviewed showed little consensus on what AI will be able to do in science, but all agreed science was on the cusp of massive change, with theoretical physics — closest to math — possibly next.

2. AI may spot clues in heart scans that doctors miss, study suggests ▶ Listen

Medical News Today — November 15, 2026

  • A study in The Lancet Digital Health of 27,382 patients in the UK and US found AI measurements of ejection fraction from cardiac MRI scans were slightly but significantly better than experienced clinicians at identifying patients at increased risk of death, over an average follow-up of more than 5 years (3,458 deaths recorded).
  • The AI tool analysed a scan in approximately 2 to 3 seconds per patient, compared with around 13 minutes for expert clinician analysis.
  • The tool had an approximately 1% error rate at patient level, and researchers propose AI as a first-line measurement tool with clinicians still reviewing and interpreting every result.

3. AI discovers a hidden heart signal that could predict sudden death ▶ Listen

ScienceDaily — November 14, 2026

  • UC Berkeley researchers trained AI on more than 440,000 electrocardiograms collected in Sweden, linked to death certificates, and tested it on additional patient records from the United States and Taiwan.
  • The AI identified a group with an annual sudden cardiac death rate of 7%, versus 4.6% for the group identified by traditional screening based on how much blood the heart pumps.
  • The system works with ordinary EKG images already routinely collected in hospitals, and the findings, published in Nature, could help physicians decide who should receive an implanted defibrillator.

5. Top scorer on South Korean tax exam confesses to cheating with AI ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 15, 2026

  • The top scorer on South Korea's national exam for aspiring tax accountants, identified by the surname Han, confessed on Sunday to using generative AI to cheat, after scoring near-perfect marks on hundreds of questions over exams in April and July.
  • Han said he took photos of exam questions with a microcamera, sent them to an AI tool, and checked the answers on a smartphone, and that he had submitted a written confession to Seoul police.
  • Prime Minister Han Seok-sook acknowledged public concerns over AI-assisted cheating ahead of South Korea's high-stakes annual college entrance exam this Thursday, with the government promising thorough oversight.

8. DUI suspect released after officer used AI in arrest report: Judge ▶ Listen

ABC News — November 13, 2026

  • Maricopa County Superior Court Commissioner Daniel Fenzel ordered Anthony Edward Rene Quidera, 37, released at his initial hearing because the Oct. 29 arrest report on Interstate 10 in Phoenix ended with the disclaimer "This report was generated with assistance from Truleo AI," and the court could not tell which allegations the trooper authored and which generative AI produced — so probable cause could not be determined at that time.
  • The trooper alleged Quidera drove 100 mph in a 65 mph zone, showed "six out of six" intoxication cues in field sobriety tests, and had several open containers in the car; DPS spokesman Raul Garcia said department policy allows AI only as a drafting aid, the trooper remains the author, and the draft must be checked against body-camera footage and reviewed by a supervisor.
  • The ruling did not end the case (an arraignment was scheduled), and it follows an Arizona Court of Appeals ruling weeks earlier ordering resentencing in a manslaughter case after a victim's sister used an AI-generated video of the slain victim at sentencing. 10. Legal challenge to UK's largest approved solar farm rejected ▶ Listen

BBC News — November 12, 2026

  • The High Court rejected a legal challenge to the UK's largest currently approved solar farm: Lincolnshire County Council and North Kesteven District Council sought to overturn the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero's April approval of Springwell Solar Farm, between Lincoln and Sleaford, arguing it would harm rural communities and landscapes, and the court dismissed their request to move forward with a judicial review.
  • Developer EDF said the 800 MW project will cover 1,280 hectares (3,163 acres) and power 180,000 homes, making a major contribution to the UK's future energy mix; a project spokesperson said it would create up to 600 jobs at peak construction plus a lifetime community benefit fund.
  • Council leaders Sean Matthews and Richard Wright said they were disappointed; a DESNZ spokesperson welcomed the decision, calling solar one of the cheapest and quickest forms of energy to build. A separate legal challenge against the Beacon Fen Solar Farm near Sleaford was announced by Lincolnshire County Council last month.
