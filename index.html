DeepSeek, Moonshot used OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra to train latest models, The Information reports

Reuters — October 29, 2026

  • Chinese AI developers DeepSeek and Moonshot AI relied heavily on outputs from OpenAI's powerful GPT-6 Astra model to train their latest models, The Information reported, citing people with direct knowledge of the matter.
  • The practice, known as distillation, has become a flashpoint for leading AI companies as US-China technology competition intensifies. Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro model, released last week, and DeepSeek's upgraded V4.1-Flash 1017 model, launched on October 17, benefited from distilling Astra, The Information said.
  • In a September 30 blog post, OpenAI said it had linked a core part of a distillation campaign that began in July to individuals associated with Moonshot AI. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google have argued the practice enables rivals to reproduce advanced capabilities more quickly and at lower cost.

Most tweens and teens use AI for schoolwork and emotional support, a new survey shows

NPR — October 28, 2026

  • A new Jed Foundation survey of more than 5,000 tweens and teens finds that 58 percent said they use chatbots for help with schoolwork, and a similar number turn to chatbots when they're sad, stressed, lonely or overwhelmed.
  • Students told NPR they use ChatGPT or Gemini to make practice questions for exams and to seek reassurance when stressed about tests; one 15-year-old said his 6-year-old brother is already talking to chatbots as well.
  • Using chatbots for companionship or when distressed was linked with worse mental health symptoms overall — higher levels of loneliness, less resilience, and a history of suicidal ideation and attempts. Researchers said it is unclear whether AI use contributes to poor mental health or whether students already in distress are more likely to turn to AI, and preliminary data suggest it is likely both.

The maths bust-up fuelling fears that AI is undermining science

BBC News — October 28, 2026

  • OpenAI said last month that its AI agents had solved one of maths' great unsolved problems, relating to the famous Navier–Stokes equations, in 88 hours. The body that runs a prize for solving the problem said it had “apparently been settled”, but that its review would take time.
  • Hours before the announcement, Tristan Buckmaster, a mathematician at New York University, went public with concerns. He and a co-researcher had been working towards the same goal and kept their drafts in OpenAI's coding tool, Codex, and he asked whether his research could have fed into OpenAI's own work, while stressing he was “not accusing anyone of anything”. OpenAI said nobody at the company, human or AI, saw the other team's work before it was published.
  • The dispute highlights a wider fear that AI could undermine faith in the scientific process, raising questions about the novelty and accuracy of research, as chatbots are known to invent things that are not true and journals face a growing volume of AI-generated distortions. AI-powered robocalls attack Talarico on data centers

CNN — October 29, 2026

  • Lone Star Liberty, a pro-Ken Paxton super PAC that received $10 million from Elon Musk, is using an AI-powered robocall campaign in Texas to attack Democratic Senate candidate James Talarico for allegedly taking money from “data center special interest” groups, according to audio recordings reviewed by CNN.
  • Unlike a traditional pre-recorded robocall, the system features an AI-generated caller named “Jennifer”, who identifies itself as a virtual staffer and is designed to interact with voters in real time. CNN's KFile identified the robocalls through Nomorobo; the calls were first detected on October 23, and the same “Jennifer” voice was used by at least two other PACs this cycle.
  • The Federal Communications Commission is considering whether to loosen its rules on political robocalls, a change that could permit large-scale unsolicited calls using artificial or prerecorded voices to reach mobile phones without prior consent. Top Democrats and consumer-rights groups oppose the petition.

Federal Reserve holds interest rates steady despite inflation concerns

USA Today — October 28, 2026

  • Federal Reserve policymakers left their benchmark rate unchanged on October 28, at a range of 3.75% to 4%, in a 10-2 vote, pausing after last month's hike to assess its effects before the November 3 midterms. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented, preferring another quarter-point hike.
  • Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said interest rates have had an effect on the artificial intelligence industry — “whether you pronounce it AI or SI” — and that present AI development is contributing to economic demand and reflects investment in the technology, though longer-term impacts are still uncertain. He has also introduced a technology task force to assess the economic impact of technologies including AI.
  • The coverage notes that high rates and uncertainty have prompted businesses to slow hiring while turning to artificial intelligence to pick up the slack: data from recruiting platform Greenhouse shows the average time to fill an open role has climbed from 41.4 days in 2022 to 61.5 days in the first eight months of 2026.

Anthropic tells California governor courts should be able to block risky AI models

Reuters — October 28, 2026

  • Anthropic told California Governor Gavin Newsom that government officials should be able to seek court orders blocking the release of AI models that pose a “serious risk” to the public, according to an October 15 letter that echoed the company's earlier policy proposals.
  • Anthropic submitted the recommendations after the governor in September convened a panel of artificial intelligence experts to develop proposals for AI safety requirements. OpenAI on October 20 also sent a list of recommendations to Newsom.
  • The company said it opposes requirements that developers build “kill switches” capable of shutting down entire AI models if a company loses control of them. Instead, Anthropic supports more targeted interventions, such as disabling a problematic AI agent or cutting off a specific customer's access to a model.

‘It's offensively cheap’: How solar power is quietly becoming bigger than nuclear

BBC News — October 26, 2026

  • The world's solar power is crossing a threshold. A decade ago, it barely registered on a global scale, providing just 1% of the world's power. In 2026, it will almost certainly increase to 10% of power, and overtake nuclear, a mainstay of electricity since the late 1970s.
  • Globally, solar electricity generation quietly overtook nuclear power in 2025, according to statistics from UK-based energy think tank Ember, and BBC analysis of Ember data over the past 12 months up to September 2026 shows it clearly outstripping nuclear, producing 13% more power.
  • The BBC sets out seven reasons for solar's success and where it might go from here. The headline link goes to a carrier page for the BBC story, which was first reported by the BBC on October 26, 2026.
