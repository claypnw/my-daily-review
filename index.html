Microsoft AI beats doctors in simulated diagnosis test

Fox News — November 1, 2026

  • Microsoft's MAI Diagnostic Orchestrator, a multi-agent system that questions and challenges itself like a panel of doctors, correctly identified 83% of 304 complex cases from The New England Journal of Medicine, against 36% for generalist physicians with reference materials; the study was published October 22 in Science.
  • In a cost-conscious setting it reached about 81% accuracy at an estimated $2,241 in diagnostic costs per case, against about $4,725 for physicians.
  • The test was a simulation, not real patients, and a clinical evaluation with Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center is planned.

How to tell if a voice recording is AI-generated

Scientific American — November 2, 2026

  • Voice cloning now needs only a few seconds of audio and a couple of clicks; in tests listeners flagged clones correctly only 60% of the time, and judged a clone and the real speaker to be the same person about 80% of the time side by side.
  • Detection now relies on audio forensics — waveform measures such as jitter and shimmer weighed together, plus inaudible watermarks hidden by some makers — because no single measure works across all cloning tools.
  • The article notes a cloned-voice fraud that persuaded an Italian bank to wire about €95 million.

Tempted to ask AI about your skin? Think again

USA Today — November 2, 2026

  • 34% of U.S. adults said in 2026 they had used an AI chatbot for at least one health reason, driven by speed and cost against an average 36.5-day wait and about $245 to see a dermatologist.
  • Nearly half of ChatGPT's responses to common skin-condition questions last year were judged inaccurate or too vague for clinical use.
  • Dermatologists report more irritant contact dermatitis and rosacea flare-ups after patients layered chatbot-recommended products.

OpenAI says its AI is getting better at doing things humans do, like shopping and meal planning

CNN — November 2, 2026

  • OpenAI announced a new internal tool for assessing how AI handles everyday tasks, and said its GPT-6 Astra model scored 93.1% at finding the right kindergarten after a move, 98% at managing community volunteers preparing for a flood, and 90.4% at creating a meal plan for a parent recently diagnosed with diabetes.
  • GPT-6 Astra scored 80% on general performance on everyday tasks, against 49.3% for GPT-5.2; the tests were built from surveys of about 4,000 ChatGPT users and task rubrics written by about 100 users.
  • The race is on to turn chatbots into personal agents: Meta's Muse agent reached 10 million U.S. downloads in its first 40 days, and OpenAI and Anthropic launched their own agent apps this fall. The headline link goes to a CNN affiliate page carrying the CNN story.

Brutal AI selloff stirs recession fears

Reuters — November 2, 2026

  • AI has propped up growth: Oxford Economics estimates AI-related effects boosted GDP growth by about 0.2 percentage point on average across six advanced economies in 2025–2026, and about a third of U.S. growth of 2.4% was AI-related; Goldman Sachs estimates the AI wealth effect added 0.4 point to U.S. consumer spending growth over the past year.
  • With the S&P 500 down 4% from its peak and the Nasdaq down 10%, a deflating AI bubble could reverse those wealth effects — stocks are a record about 47% of U.S. households' financial assets.
  • With underlying growth only about 0.5%–1.0%, the piece warns that reversal could tip several major economies into recession. This is a Reuters commentary piece.

Asia's Factory Activity Buoyed by AI Demand, but Cracks Emerge

The Wall Street Journal — November 2, 2026

  • S&P Global PMI data released Monday show strong demand for AI hardware and components continued to support Asian manufacturing, even as Taiwan, Japan and South Korea slowed — Taiwan's PMI eased to 54.9 from 56.7, Japan hit a seven-month low, and South Korea posted its slowest output growth this year.
  • The AI wave is also a strain: supplier delivery times lengthened as firms struggled to procure inputs, with S&P Global citing Middle East conflict, freight delays and AI-driven supply-chain pressure.
  • Optimism rests on hopes the semiconductor demand “supercycle” continues.

Data Center Restrictions Make the Ballot in Dozens of US Cities

Bloomberg — November 2, 2026

  • Voters in dozens of U.S. counties and cities will vote on policies that would tax, regulate or even ban AI infrastructure, in more than 40 data center-related measures on the November 3 midterm ballot, per a Bloomberg analysis of Ballotpedia data — including measures in Nevada and in California, where Alhambra, a Los Angeles suburb, could vote to ban data centers outright.
  • Opposition is surging: a Quinnipiac poll in September found 72% of voters would oppose a new data center in their community, up from 65% in March, and at least 45 projects worth $68 billion were disrupted by local opposition in the second quarter.
  • In Boulder City, Nevada, activists blocked a $3 billion data center on a city-owned tortoise preserve, but the developer then won approval on neighboring federal land — showing local rules do not cross jurisdictional lines. The headline link goes to Insurance Journal, which carries the Bloomberg story.

Flash flooding forces evacuations across Colorado, Arizona, New Mexico

Associated Press — November 1, 2026

  • Heavy rainfall produced flash flooding and mandatory evacuations across the Southwest on Sunday, November 1, with as much as 4 inches of rain expected, fueled by Pacific storm systems — “two or three months of rain in a span of a couple of days,” a National Weather Service meteorologist in Tucson said.
  • In Gila County, officials ordered the total evacuation of Gisela, a community of several hundred residents along Tonto Creek, near Payson; communities in central Arizona, including Camp Verde, also issued mandatory evacuations, and several highways were closed.
  • Arizona Gov. Katie Hobbs said state air rescue crews were assisting with rooftop evacuations in Sedona, where residents near Oak Creek were ordered to leave as floodwaters trapped people in their homes. The headline link goes to a carrier page carrying the Associated Press story.

Dry conditions ahead after Arizona hit with historic flooding

Arizona's Family — November 2, 2026

  • Dry weather returns to Arizona after days of widespread rain, and flooding concerns will continue through at least midweek for many locations.

TotalEnergies spending more on US renewables as others exit, says CEO

Reuters — November 2, 2026

  • TotalEnergies is spending slightly more in the U.S. than originally planned and taking on more renewable projects as other developers seek to exit, CEO Patrick Pouyanne said Monday, calling the market “promising.”
  • A 2025 U.S. tax law accelerated the phase-out of wind and solar tax credits — most new projects must have begun construction by July 2026 or enter service by the end of 2027 to qualify — and developers, including TotalEnergies, are accelerating projects to benefit.
  • Pouyanne said fear that the market will collapse around 2028 or 2030 creates an opportunity for TotalEnergies to acquire projects in development and handle construction.
