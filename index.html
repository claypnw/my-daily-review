OpenAI says human intent at center of updated AI model guidelines

Reuters — October 29, 2026

  • OpenAI on Thursday updated its Model Spec, a public roadmap for how it wants its AI models to behave, reorganizing the framework around five core values as AI systems become more capable.
  • The five values are faithfulness to human intent, harm minimization, honesty and transparency, human agency, and care. Faithfulness to human intent is the foundation, requiring models to understand what users, developers and OpenAI intend and value, rather than simply following instructions literally.
  • OpenAI said current models do not yet fully reflect the updated Model Spec and that it is exploring new training methods to help models internalize the principles. The move echoes rival Anthropic's Constitutional AI approach.

AI makes Britain's biggest woodland bat survey possible

BBC News — October 30, 2026

  • The largest woodland bat survey ever carried out in Britain would have been impossible without AI, according to the scientists behind it, the BBC reported on October 30. Over three years, acoustic recorders in 310 woodlands logged 98 million bat calls, a volume the team says would have taken more than three decades to sort by hand.
  • Researchers fixed recording devices to poles among the trees and captured bats' echolocation calls night after night, producing 90,000 hours of audio. Open-source AI tools then picked out and classified the calls.
  • The results include hotspots for the rare, red-listed Barbastelle in south-west England, in Somerset, Devon, Cornwall and Dorset. The headline link goes to a carrier page for the BBC story.

AI hardware can be hacked so chatbots provide dangerous answers, research shows

CNN — October 29, 2026

  • The hardware underpinning major AI models can be hacked so that chatbots provide faulty and dangerous answers, according to new research shared exclusively with CNN by Eclypsium, a firm staffed by security engineers who previously worked at Intel.
  • Researchers vastly increased the probability an AI model assigns to a Chinese invasion of Taiwan by remotely tampering with the “weights” the model uses, and a tampered model told a user seeking advice for chest pain to stay at home instead of going to the hospital.
  • Eclypsium demonstrated the attack on a computing platform made by Nvidia, and said the scenario is not unique to Nvidia. Two independent experts who reviewed the findings for CNN said they were credible and important.

Google AI wouldn't say who won the 2020 election

USA Today — October 29, 2026

  • If you asked Google on Wednesday, October 28, who won the 2020 election, “Joe Biden” wasn't the answer. Google AI pointed instead to certified reports from the Federal Election Commission. When asked about 2016 or 2024, it correctly answered Donald Trump. As of Thursday, October 29, the glitch was fixed.
  • Google said its systems verify election responses using news articles or AP data for recent elections and historic records for older ones, and that there was a technical gap with 2020 — the same is true for 2022 Senate races, which it is also working to fix.
  • The glitch comes less than a week before the 2026 midterm elections, amid fears AI will be used to promote election interference or misinformation.

Republicans lead AI-generated campaign ad spending boom, study finds

USA Today — October 30, 2026

  • The Wesleyan Media Project has identified more than $187 million in spending on at least 289 unique AI-generated or AI-assisted ads this year as of October 29 — up from barely $50,000 in 2024.
  • Experts said the 2026 campaign cycle marks the widest-yet use of AI, from ads to outreach where bots can reply to voters' text responses. Of the AI-generated ads counted through mid-October, only 27% contain a disclaimer.
  • Republican candidates, a Republican Party committee or a Republican-leaning group were responsible for just over 90% of spending on ads generated or enhanced by AI through late October, the Wesleyan report found.

With days to go before the midterms, data shows the U.S. economy is doing well

NPR — October 29, 2026

  • The U.S. economy grew faster in the summer compared to the spring, driven by consumer spending as well as investments in AI. Gross domestic product grew at an annual rate of 3.4% July through September, up from 2.2% in the previous quarter.
  • A big driver was strong investment from AI companies, which continue to build out data centers and spend big money developing the technology.
  • Many Americans still feel gloomy about the economy as inflation raises day-to-day costs. Prices rose 3.6% in September from a year earlier, and economist Mark Zandi warned the growth feels delicate and fragile.

Wall Street's AI fears slash Google value by $500bn

BBC News — October 29, 2026

  • Google's parent Alphabet lost more than $500 billion in stock market value in a single day, after investors reacted to a quarterly update showing huge AI spending beginning to bite into its most important business. Alphabet ended Thursday worth $3.46 trillion, down from $3.97 trillion, as shares fell almost 13% to a six-month low.
  • Revenue rose by a quarter to $127.6 billion, helped by cloud sales that more than doubled to almost $31 billion. But Alphabet reported negative free cash flow for only the second time since it became a public company, with a shortfall exceeding $9 billion, and almost all the extra spending was directed at AI.
  • At the heart of the concern is Google Search: AI-generated summaries now answer questions directly rather than sending users onward, reducing advertisements shown alongside results — and search advertising remains Alphabet's largest source of income. The headline link goes to a carrier page for the BBC story.

Is AI this holiday shopping season's Secret Santa?

USA Today — October 30, 2026

  • Sixty-five percent of respondents in the Visa 2026 Holiday Spending Shift Survey said they'd let AI assist their holiday shopping, with 24% saying they're more comfortable with the technology than last year. The survey asked more than 1,000 U.S. adults.
  • Shoppers were most comfortable letting AI compare prices (61%), check availability (59%), find discounts (57%) and suggest gift ideas (54%). Only 23% said they would trust generative AI to handle a payment transaction.
  • The National Retail Federation forecasts holiday sales growth of 4.2% from last year, while PwC projects holiday gift spending will dip 2% and Simon Kucher projects Americans will spend 5.5% less this holiday season.

Amazon falls 7% after results despite AWS growth

Associated Press — October 29, 2026

  • Amazon shares fell 7% in after-hours trading when it reported third-quarter results on October 29, as investors focused on cash burn amid expanding AI investments, according to the Associated Press. Amazon Web Services growth accelerated to 39.3%, its fastest rate in nearly five years.
  • AWS sales reached about $46 billion in the quarter ended September 30. Net sales rose 12% to $201.94 billion, below the $202.2 billion analysts expected, and net income was $20.69 billion, or $1.89 per share.
  • Chief executive Andy Jassy said the company expects to spend $220 billion during 2026, most of it on building data centers for artificial intelligence. The headline link goes to a carrier page carrying the Associated Press report.

Semiconductors, data centers fueling development in Phoenix

The Arizona Republic — October 27, 2026

  • Semiconductor industry investment and demand for data centers are fueling Phoenix's reputation for economic growth. Metro Phoenix has been the top market for semiconductor investment since 2020 and ranks fourth globally in data center operating capacity, according to the Emerging Trends in Real Estate report from the Urban Land Institute and PwC.
  • The Phoenix region now has about 189,000 semiconductor-related jobs. The Arizona Commerce Authority reports the state has seen about $319 billion in semiconductor industry investment since 2020, the lion's share from Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.'s campus in north Phoenix.
  • The Phoenix metro has about 1.6 gigawatts of data center capacity under construction, and the industry has fueled about $7.8 billion of investment from companies buying and building data center facilities.

Can states force social media sites to carry warning labels?

Reuters — October 29, 2026

  • Internet industry trade group NetChoice is challenging laws in multiple states that would require social media companies to warn minors about excessive social media use, in a growing First Amendment battle over compelled speech.
  • California's law AB 56, slated to take effect January 1, will require platforms such as Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok to caution children and teens that social media is associated with significant mental health harms and has not been proven safe for young users, at first login, again after three hours of use, and at least once every hour after that.
  • NetChoice is challenging a similar law in Colorado and won an injunction last year to stop it taking effect; that decision is now on appeal. A case against Minnesota is also pending in federal court.

SEG Solar secures land for planned $950 million cell plant

Reuters — October 29, 2026

  • SEG Solar said on Thursday it had secured land for a planned $950 million solar cell facility near Houston, Texas, that would add 4 gigawatts of annual manufacturing capacity.
  • The heterojunction (HJT) cell plant at its Tomball Industrial Campus is expected to begin operations in two 2-GW phases, with completion targeted for 2028 and 2029. It would sit beside SEG's existing 4 GW module plant and its planned 4.6 GW module facility.
  • The project is part of the U.S. solar panel maker's push to build a vertically integrated solar manufacturing supply chain amid growing demand for domestically produced equipment, potentially helping customers meet domestic-content requirements.
