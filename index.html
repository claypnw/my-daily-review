Reuters — November 19, 2026

8. For some, diet significantly swings cholesterol levels, study finds ▶ Listen

Medical Xpress — November 18, 2026

  • A Stanford Medicine-led study of nearly 21,000 women, published November 18 in JACC: Advances, found higher saturated fat intake raised LDL cholesterol more in women with a genetic propensity for high cholesterol.
  • The top 25% of participants with that genetic propensity who ate more saturated fat were significantly likelier to have cardiovascular events such as heart attacks and strokes, while for the remaining 75% higher saturated fat did not markedly increase that risk.
  • Senior author Shoa Clarke said even for people on the low end of risk, eating more saturated fat gave no protective benefit, and that healthy cholesterol levels reported by some people on high-fat ketogenic diets fit the influence of underlying genetics.

12. Florida's stormwater ponds become floating solar farms as costs fall and demand rises ▶ Listen

Associated Press — November 17, 2026

  • Some of Florida's tens of thousands of retention ponds are being used to generate solar power, with floating photovoltaics - panels mounted on buoyant structures - becoming less expensive since the technology emerged two decades ago.
  • The median reported cost of floating solar fell from $2.41 per watt in 2015 to $1.25 per watt in 2024, a decline of about 48%, though it still costs more upfront than land-based solar and its environmental impact is not fully understood.
  • In November 2025 Orlando Utilities Commission launched the 2-megawatt Conway Road array, more than 3,400 panels and the state's largest floating solar installation; Florida has an estimated 76,000 stormwater ponds.
