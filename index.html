1. Big Pharma, with eye on Anthropic, says AI is shrinking drug discovery timelines ▶ Listen

Reuters — November 20, 2026

  • Artificial intelligence has sped up early-stage drug development at some of the world's largest pharmaceutical companies, cutting by as much as half the time it takes to produce new molecules and move them into the clinic, according to their top scientists.
  • GSK, Bristol Myers Squibb, Novartis, Eli Lilly, Novo Nordisk, AstraZeneca and Sanofi say they now use AI for designing molecules, sorting the most promising ones, and identifying safety risks; Sanofi said AI saved at least a year on its asthma treatment lunsekimig, and Novo Nordisk cut molecule design cycles from around eight to three.
  • Anthropic is gearing up to be a rival, partner or both to the drugmakers, with its own laboratory and its Claude Science software; Lilly's Aliza Apple said claims that the technology can cure all disease overstate current capabilities.

10. The connection between high-fat ketogenic diets and weight loss ▶ Listen

Medical Xpress — November 19, 2026

  • New findings reported in Cell Metabolism suggest dietary composition and genetic differences may be overlooked influences on metabolic responses to ketogenic diets in mice; in genetically susceptible mice, a very-low-protein version triggered liver stress and inflammatory responses that suppressed appetite.
  • Mice fed a ketogenic diet with just 5% protein reduced food intake by about 40% after four weeks and lost weight, while mice on a similar diet with 10% protein ate more and gained weight, even though both groups produced ketones.
  • The team identified two genes, NNT and NLRP12; because the study was done in mice, the findings cannot be directly applied to humans, first author Zhaoyue Zhang said, and the low-protein mice also developed fatty liver and lost lean mass.
