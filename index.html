USDA to launch AI tool to check crop production data after errors▶ Listen

Reuters — November 6, 2026

  • The US Department of Agriculture will use an artificial intelligence tool it built to check data in its monthly crop production report due on November 10, after a series of data missteps eroded farmers' and traders' trust in its forecasts.
  • Officials determined the AI tool would have flagged the errors in September's corn data. A computer file had incorrectly combined data on irrigated and non-irrigated corn acres, raising the yield estimate by about 3% in Nebraska and lowering it by about 2% in Kansas.
  • The new AI tool will add to, not replace, the agency's data validation, Under Secretary Scott Hutchins said. The chair of the agricultural statistics board said the September error was an isolated occurrence that proper validation should have caught.

Oil-rich UAE makes massive AI bet as Sam Altman unveils ambitious plan to transform energy industry▶ Listen

Fox News — November 6, 2026

  • At the opening of the ADIPEC energy conference in Abu Dhabi, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman joined senior UAE officials for the announcement of a major partnership aimed at bringing advanced AI into the energy industry, between ADNOC, OpenAI and Abu Dhabi-based technology company G42.
  • The ADNOC partnership is intended to connect advanced AI systems across the company's operations, from oilfield development and reservoir management to refining, logistics and commercial trading. One initial application will bring together decades of information from thousands of oil wells, with the goal of completing complex development plans in hours rather than months.
  • Asked to identify his biggest challenge, Altman answered with a single word: energy. AI companies need vast quantities of electricity to operate data centers, while energy producers are increasingly looking to AI to improve efficiency, optimize production and identify vulnerabilities before they disrupt supplies.

Robust October hiring and drop in unemployment defy expectations▶ Listen

USA Today — November 6, 2026

  • U.S. employers added a surprising 165,000 jobs in October, the Labor Department estimated Nov. 6, surpassing forecasters' expectations. The unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1% in October, from 4.2% the month before.

SpaceX unveils plan for massive data center in Cochise County▶ Listen

The Arizona Republic — November 6, 2026

  • SpaceX wants to build and operate a 747-acre data center campus near the Willcox Playa in Cochise County, a plan the company outlined for nearly 300 people who packed an elementary school auditorium on Nov. 4. If built, it would become one of the biggest data centers in the country, and the biggest in Arizona, demanding more than 6 gigawatts of power when completed.
  • According to the permit application, the campus would be dry-cooled and pump no groundwater for industrial use. The planned facility would demand about 2.5 times more power than all data centers in Maricopa County combined, and would invest $3.6 billion in grid upgrades.
  • The project would reduce residents' electricity bills, lower property taxes by 20% and create some 1,000 permanent jobs, SpaceX's head of energy and supercomputer department said at the presentation. The county's Planning and Zoning Division will hold a public hearing for the project on Dec. 9.

How China's clean-tech machine is reshaping the global climate fight▶ Listen

Reuters — November 6, 2026

  • China sent $132 billion in clean technology and investment to the developing world last year, by far the largest contribution to carbon-dioxide reduction of any country, and nearly equal to the $137 billion in climate finance that wealthy nations provided to poorer ones in 2024.
  • China took a different approach from rich-world climate finance, funneling billions in subsidies into its own green-energy industries. The result has been massive and rapid market-share gains in solar panels, batteries and battery minerals, electric vehicles and wind turbines.
  • Nearly two thirds of China's exports of solar panels, and the cells needed to make them, went to the developing world last year, according to Reuters analysis of customs data. Those exports alone will help the Global South avoid 257 million metric tons of carbon dioxide emissions per year once deployed.
