What impact AI is having on the economy

Up to 5 stories. 4 found today.

IMF and OECD warn AI could widen the gap between rich and poor countries

Reuters — October 22, 2026

  • Full use of AI could add up to 0.5% a year to world growth, the IMF estimates — but the measured productivity gains so far are much smaller, and workplace use trails consumer use.
  • About 40% of jobs worldwide are exposed to AI; poorer countries, smaller companies, and younger workers risk missing out without power, networks, and skills.
  • The same report round notes Barclays cutting about 5,000 jobs this year as it leans on AI, and eBay reported laying off 9% of its workforce.

Barclays cuts 5,000 jobs leaning on AI; the Fed sees early signs in its districts

Morningstar (MarketWatch) — October 22, 2026

  • Barclays has cut 1,000 jobs and will cut 4,000 more by year's end, about 5% of its workforce, as AI takes over back-office tasks.
  • The Federal Reserve's October Beige Book: five of twelve districts report some firms trimmed staff, delayed hiring, or expect less hiring after AI raised productivity.
  • AI is the leading stated reason in U.S. job-cut announcements this year — 120,136 positions, 21% of all cuts (Challenger, Gray & Christmas). U.S. unemployment is still low at 4.2%.

IMF: AI spending is holding world growth near 3% — and a "slow-moving tsunami" is coming for jobs

PYMNTS — October 12, 2026 (IMF and World Bank annual meetings, Bangkok)

  • Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva: AI investment is a key reason world growth holds around 3% despite the energy supply shock.
  • IMF research: about 40% of jobs worldwide will be augmented, downgraded, or eliminated by AI — up to 60% in advanced economies.
  • One study Georgieva cited: 10% of U.S. jobs now require AI skills; routine jobs are shrinking while low-paying construction and data-center jobs grow.

Stanford's October numbers: young workers in AI-exposed jobs keep sliding

Stanford Digital Economy Lab — October 21, 2026

  • Early-career employment in the most AI-exposed jobs is down 3.7% from a year ago, and down 12% since ChatGPT launched in November 2022; the least-exposed group is up 8% over the same span.
  • The effect is spreading to workers aged 26–30: down 2.8% year over year in the most-exposed jobs.
  • In the lab's wider set of measures, there is still no strong sign of an economy-wide transformation.

What impact AI is having on states

Up to 5 stories. 4 found today.

Arizona has paused new data center tax breaks for three years

Fjord Journal — October 16, 2026

  • The budget Governor Hobbs signed pauses new applications for state data center tax relief from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2029. It does not stop construction itself.
  • In the governor's race, Republican candidate Andy Biggs calls for ending the tax breaks and subsidies outright, with protections for other power and water users; No Labels candidate Teri Ann Hourihan proposes pausing new applications while impacts are assessed.
  • Data centers are also an issue in Senate, House, and governor races in other states (WIRED's October 11 overview).

After years of competing for data centers, states changed course in 2026

Her Agenda — October 22, 2026

  • Virginia now taxes data centers per kilowatt-hour — about $600 million a year expected — while its sales tax break, worth over $1 billion a year, stays for now. Maine ended two tax break programs for new data centers.
  • Texas: the governor ordered an audit of data center grid connections in August; about 474 gigawatts of connection requests are pending, roughly 90% from data centers.
  • Oklahoma and South Dakota passed ratepayer protection laws, so big power users pay their own grid costs. Georgia is the exception: its tax breaks stayed.

Towns and counties are pausing data centers on their own

SAVRN moratorium tracker — October 12, 2026

  • Blaine County, Idaho: a 182-day emergency pause on data center permits, passed October 7, citing water and energy use.
  • Lawrence Township, New Jersey: an ordinance to ban data centers town-wide was introduced October 5. West Seneca, New York: a one-year pause passed October 4.
  • Frederick, Maryland is debating a two-year pause on data center rule changes.

AI data centers are on the ballot in seven states this November

JASTORM (Fox News AI Newsletter) — October 8, 2026

  • Voters in California, Texas, Florida, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Michigan, and Georgia will decide measures tied to AI data center construction.
  • The measures would let local governments approve or deny projects; supporters point to jobs and investment, opponents to energy use and community impact.
